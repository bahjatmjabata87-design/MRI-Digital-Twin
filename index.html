Synthetic MRI computed live from real brain anatomy: grey matter, white matter and CSF maps of the MNI ICBM152 2009a template (a standard average of 152 adult brain MRI scans). Brightness is relative spin-echo signal at the chosen settings.

Spin-echo contrast

TR (repetition time)
TE (echo time)

Off: the brightest tissue is always shown as white, to show contrast clearly. On: brightness follows the true signal and image noise is added, so weak settings look dark and grainy, and 3 T looks cleaner than 1.5 T.

Signal equation used

S = PD × (1 − e−TR/T1) × e−TE/T2

Noise model (realistic mode): Gaussian noise in two channels with a magnitude image (Rician noise). Noise is set so that signal-to-noise ratio roughly doubles from 1.5 T to 3 T, a common simplification; real SNR also depends on coils, voxel size, bandwidth and averages.

Short TR increases T1 differences; long TE increases T2 differences. Real sequences (fast spin echo, inversion recovery, gradient echo) behave differently; this model is for learning the principle.

About this project

Version 1.1 of an educational MRI simulator for radiography and medical students. It has three modules: a contrast lab, a brain positioning and slice-planning lab, and an MRI suite showing the four ACR safety zones with a projectile demonstration. The planning lab lets students position a virtual patient, plan axial, coronal or sagittal slices on three-plane localizers, change scan parameters and see the resulting images and scan time update live, then check their scan against a task list.

Positioning and planning lab (model notes)

  • Positioning: head first, supine, head coil with foam pads, hearing protection, laser landmark on the nasion. Axial brain slices are planned parallel to the AC–PC line and cover the vertex to the foramen magnum; coronal slices are perpendicular to the AC–PC line; sagittal slices are parallel to the midline (falx).
  • Head anatomy: the 3D grey matter, white matter and CSF maps of the MNI ICBM152 2009a template at 2 mm. The skull (no signal) and a scalp fat layer are simple illustrative shells, not real anatomy. AC and PC markers were located approximately on the template (AC ≈ 0, 2, −4 mm; PC ≈ 0, −28, −2 mm). Each new patient has a random head tilt.
  • Scan time = TR × phase-encoding steps × NEX ÷ echo train length (× number of acquisitions if all slices do not fit in one TR). Relative SNR ∝ voxel volume × √(phase steps × NEX). Pixel size = FOV ÷ matrix.
  • Scalp fat: T1/T2 382/68 ms at 3 T (de Bazelaire et al. 2004); 1.5 T values approximate. Fat is kept bright on fast spin echo to mimic the J-coupling effect.
  • Artefacts shown: wrap-around (aliasing) along the phase-encoding direction when the FOV is too small, motion ghosts without immobilisation, and signal fall-off far from the isocentre. All are simplified.

Brain images

The contrast lab uses the grey and white matter maps of the MNI ICBM152 2009a nonlinear symmetric template, as distributed with the open-source nilearn library. CSF is estimated as the brain volume not classed as grey or white matter. Safety content

Zone definitions follow the American College of Radiology (ACR) Manual on MR Safety. Object labels follow the MR Safe, MR Conditional and MR Unsafe terms of ASTM F2503. Objects in the projectile test are examples; always check the actual item's labelling and your site's MR safety policy.

Disclaimer

This simulator is for education only. It is not a medical device, does not model any specific scanner, and must not be used for clinical decisions, patient screening or site planning.
